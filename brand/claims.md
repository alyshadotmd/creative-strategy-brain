# Claims

What can and cannot be said about this brand and its products. The most asserted document in the brain — none of it is observed or inferred. Someone in the business set these rules, and breaking one is the failure with consequences outside the ad account.

Read by `script-writing`, `briefing`, `creative-qa` and `creative-analysis`. A claim check is blocking: anything not permitted here does not ship, regardless of how well the ad performs in review.

Re-confirm whenever the product changes, the brand's legal position changes, or a platform updates its advertising policy. A stale claims list is worse than none, because it gets trusted.

## Can say

One row per claim, in the exact wording that is approved. An approved *idea* is not an approved claim — the wording is the claim.

| Claim, as worded | Applies to | Backed by | Conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |

*Backed by* is the study, certification, test, or internal data that substantiates it. A claim with nothing in this column is not approved yet, whoever said it was.

*Conditions* is anything that must accompany it — a disclaimer, a timeframe, a qualifying population, a required asterisk.

## Cannot say

| Claim | Why it's off-limits |
|---|---|

Reasons worth separating, because they behave differently: regulated category language, competitor comparison, unsubstantiated outcome, guarantee or absolute, medical or health framing, platform policy.

## Required disclosures

Anything that must appear on the creative itself — on screen, in the caption, or spoken. Note where it must appear and for how long, since a disclosure that flashes for half a second has not been made.

## Creator and partner rules

What someone speaking on the brand's behalf may and may not say, including how a personal experience must be framed, what counts as a testimonial, and the disclosure a paid partnership requires.

## Open questions

Claims someone wants to make that have not been cleared. Keep them here rather than in the can-say table — an unanswered question in the wrong column becomes an approved claim by accident.
