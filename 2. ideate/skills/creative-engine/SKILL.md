# ⚙️ Creative Strategy Engine

*A systematic framework for generating direct response ad concepts.*

---

This is the complete process from understanding your product and customer all the way down to the finished ad concept. It's a full map you can navigate to get to one really intentional ad, or hundreds of ads that cover a full funnel and are mapped strategically. It applies to any brand, any product, any campaign.

You come to this document with a goal: one ad, or many. Usually you enter at the top. Sometimes you enter in the middle, with a format or an angle or a hook you already want to try; there's a section for that below. Either way, the process is this map, and a concept isn't finished until it has walked all of it.

This skill is brand-agnostic by design. It describes the shape of each step, not what any step should contain for a given brand. Every specific comes from the brain's research folders and the brand in front of you — never from this document.

## The map

```
INPUTS  (pull from 1. research/)
   product catalog · VOC · brand context · competitors
        │
Step 1  ANCHOR on a pain or desire
        │  research surfaces it, product validates it
Step 2  PERSONA                                   → 1. research/customer/personas/
        │  group the anchor by life context
Step 3  AWARENESS STAGE
        │  where the persona is when the ad finds them
Step 4  TRIGGER MOMENT
        │  the concrete moment that moves them to the next stage
Step 5  MESSAGING ANGLE                           → 2. ideate/messaging-angles/
        │  the strategy the ad uses to make its case
Step 6  THE OFFER
        │  everything above, synthesized into one statement in their words
Step 7  VISUAL FORMAT                             → 2. ideate/visual-formats/
        │  the container that carries the offer
Step 8  HOOK                                      → 2. ideate/hooks/
        │  the opening expression: text and visual
        ▼
AD CONCEPT  → pipeline/concepts.md  → a concept folder in pipeline/
```

One really intentional ad. Or hundreds of ads that cover a full funnel, mapped strategically.

## The inputs: product and customer

Everything starts from two inputs, both produced by research and pulled from `1. research/`:

- **The product:** the facts-only catalog in `brand/products/`. What it is, does, contains, costs, includes; its variations. What may be said about it is in `brand/claims.md` — if a claim isn't permitted there, an ad can't make it.
- **The customer:** tagged VOC from `1. research/customer/` (reviews), with source noted on every quote so you know which awareness stages a quote can honestly feed. Plus brand context, competitor research from `1. research/competitors/`, and customer psychology as depth demands.

The engine consumes research; it never redoes it.

## Step 1: Anchor on a pain or desire

Any product gets found in two ways: through a pain (a problem they have) or a desire (something they want). Choose one for this run. Research surfaces the candidates: the pains and desires that show up across the VOC in the customer's own words. The product validates the pick: can we credibly solve for it? One lane per run; if two pains keep pulling at you, that's two runs.

## Step 2: Find the persona

**Pull from and write to:** `1. research/customer/personas/`.

Group the anchor by life context. A persona is a group of people who share the pain or desire AND a life context — their role, their routine, their responsibilities, the constraints they live inside, what they've tried, what they've accepted. Same pain, different lives means different personas: two people can share a symptom and nothing else about how it shows up, what they blame, or what they'd believe. The test: if two "personas" would get the same stories told to them, they're one persona.

Go deep enough that you could write from inside this person's day. You'll need that for the trigger moment.

## Step 3: Choose the awareness stage

The stage is where the persona is when the ad finds them: Unaware, Problem-Aware, Solution-Aware, Product-Aware, or Most-Aware. Each stage has a job for the ad: make the problem exist (Unaware), validate and agitate it (Problem-Aware), position the category (Solution-Aware), win the comparison (Product-Aware), close (Most-Aware).

**Funnel strategy:** start Product-Aware, the people actively evaluating options. Add Most-Aware only for intentional pushes: a sale, a limited offer, clearing a SKU. Once Product-Aware works, scale upward into Solution-Aware, then Problem-Aware, then Unaware; the upper funnel is where new customers come from. Never let Most-Aware dominate outside sale periods.

One honesty check before moving on: some people can't be met at some stages. Someone who already bought and quit a competitor can't honestly be spoken to as Unaware. Note it and pick a stage that's true.

## Step 4: Find the trigger moment

A trigger moment is the specific, concrete moment in the persona's life when the pain or desire stops being background and becomes something they act on. It's an event, not a feeling. It has a place, a time, usually other people, and a consequence. It's the thing that moves the persona from the stage you chose in Step 3 to the next one:

- Unaware → Problem-Aware: the moment the problem gets named, noticed, or costs them something they can't ignore
- Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware: the moment they decide the problem is worth fixing and start looking
- Solution-Aware → Product-Aware: the moment a current approach fails them, or a specific option enters their view
- Product-Aware → Most-Aware: the moment the reason to act now appears — an offer, a deadline, a final proof point

The ad's job is to be that moment, recreate it, or meet the persona right inside it. Anchoring on the trigger is what makes an ad feel like it was made for one person on one day instead of for a demographic.

Where to find them: the VOC is full of them — read for the story a customer tells about the day they decided to do something, not just what they said about the product. The persona's daily routine is the other source: walk through their day and look for the points where the pain surfaces in front of other people, costs them something, or collides with a solution that didn't work.

The test: could you shoot it? A trigger moment you can picture as a scene is usable. One that's just a restatement of the pain ("they feel bad about the problem") is not a moment yet — keep going until it has a where, a when, and a what-happened.

Different trigger moments for the same persona and stage are different concepts. Log each one.

## Step 5: Choose the messaging angle

**Pull from:** `2. ideate/messaging-angles/`.

The angle is the strategy the ad uses to make its case — the specific reason this product, for this persona, in this moment. It's usually one product truth from the catalog, chosen because it answers what the trigger moment just made urgent: the thing they don't have time for, the thing they just tried that failed, the thing they're now afraid of, the thing they didn't know to want.

The strategy is reusable; the angle is specific. The check: can this angle do this stage's job, and does it speak to this trigger moment, without contortion? If you're bending it to fit, change the angle or change the trigger.

The angle's core truth stays the same wherever it runs; only the expression changes. That's what makes one angle able to serve several stages when you're producing in volume.

## Step 6: Write the offer

The offer is where the map so far collapses into one statement. Take the pain or desire, the persona, the stage, the trigger moment, and the angle, and write what this product means for this person, in this moment, in words they would use.

There's a meaningful distinction between selling and offering. Selling describes the product: what it is, what it contains, what it's made of. Offering names what the product does for this specific person's life: the outcome they were chasing when the trigger moment hit, framed through the angle, said the way they'd say it to a friend.

A strong offer statement does three things at once: it carries the angle (the product truth), it names the persona's specific outcome (not a generic benefit), and it belongs to the trigger moment (it's an answer to what just happened). If you can read the offer and not know who it's for or why now, it's a tagline, not an offer.

Write it as one or two sentences. It sets up everything that follows: the format has to carry it, the hook has to open it.

## Step 7: Choose the visual format

**Pull from:** `2. ideate/visual-formats/`.

Format is the container for the offer. Match the container's purpose to the stage's job and to the trigger moment: formats that reveal for stages that reveal a problem, formats that prove for stages that prove superiority, formats that can stage a scene when the trigger moment is the ad. Any format can work at any stage when the messaging is right; the library's notes tell you where each one lives naturally. Executing one offer across multiple formats gives you more chances to connect: different people stop for different things.

## Step 8: Write the hook

**Pull from:** `2. ideate/hooks/` (openers — spoken-flow lines for video, written lines for statics).

The hook is the opening expression of the offer, at this stage, in this format. Text and visual together: the words that stop the scroll and the frames that earn the next second. When the trigger moment is strong, the hook usually lives inside it — the first second of the ad is the first second of the moment. The swipe collections are there to draw from.

**Pain or desire + persona + stage + trigger moment + angle + offer + format + hook: that's a full ad concept.** Log it as one row in `pipeline/concepts.md`, open its folder from `pipeline/_template/`, and write `overview.md`. It is then ready for the create skills.

## Entering in the middle

Usually you enter at the top. Sometimes you arrive mid-map with a component already in hand. That's allowed; entering mid-process doesn't skip the steps, it just changes the order you walk them.

- **A trigger moment you noticed:** Step 4 arrived early — often from a customer story or your own observation. Name the persona it belongs to and the stage transition it drives, then walk forward. Drop a note in `1. research/customer/` so the research catches up to what you noticed.
- **A visual format you want to try:** Step 7 is answered. Work backwards: which stage does this container naturally serve, which trigger moment could it stage, and which persona and angle give it something worth saying? A format without an offer is an empty container.
- **A messaging angle you want to try:** Step 5 is answered. Check it against a real persona, find the trigger moment it answers, write the offer, then find the stage where the angle works without contortion.
- **A persona your research didn't surface:** Step 2 arrived early. Go deep enough to find its trigger moments, then walk forward normally. Add it to `1. research/customer/personas/`.
- **A hook you think is smart:** Step 8 arrived first, which is backwards, and that's fine. A hook implies an angle, usually a stage, and often a trigger moment; name them explicitly. If you can't say who it's for, what moment it meets, and what it's doing, it's a clever line, not an ad concept yet. Save it to `2. ideate/hooks/` either way.

## One ad or many

One goal, one pass through the map, one concept: that's a complete use of this engine.

When the goal is volume ("cover the funnel for this persona," "ten concepts for the sale"), run more passes: one persona has many trigger moments, one trigger moment can take several angles, one angle expresses differently at each stage it honestly serves, and one offer can ride multiple formats. Every concept gets a row in `pipeline/concepts.md` so the batch stays visible and the coverage gaps show — which personas have no upper-funnel concepts, which stages have only one trigger moment, which angles have never been tested.

## The loop

The engine is stage 2 of the flywheel: research (`1. research`) produces its inputs, creation (`3. create`) receives its concepts, and analysis (`4. analyze`) feeds learnings back into the libraries. A trigger moment that keeps converting gets written back into the persona; an angle that keeps winning gets its entry expanded; a format that keeps dying at a stage gets its notes corrected; a hook that gets quoted back goes in the swipe file. A library that never grows means you stopped looking.
