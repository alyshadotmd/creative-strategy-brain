# Creative Strategy Dictionary

Definitions for creative strategy, marketing, and business terms these skills use. `persona-finder` and `creative-engine` use these words exactly as defined here. Alphabetical. Add new terms as they come up; keep definitions tight, one paragraph, with an example when it helps show the distinction from a neighboring term (e.g. Audience vs. Persona vs. Micro-persona).

## Audience

The identity that unites them. A role, interest, or self-identity people would actually claim. Example: "Moms," "light sleepers," "performance marketers." An ad at this depth is category-relevant but interchangeable with a competitor's.

## Cohort

A group of customers or users who share a defining starting point (e.g. signed up the same month, launched from the same campaign) and are analyzed together over time to see how that group behaves.

## Customer segment

The slice of the audience you're actually selling to, qualified by attributes that change what you'd say: life stage, behavior, situation, budget. Example: "Moms of teenagers." An ad at this depth is relevant, it narrows the room.

## Demand capture

Marketing or advertising aimed at converting demand that already exists, people actively searching or ready to buy, rather than creating new demand from scratch (demand generation).

## Demographic

Who they are on paper. Observable, census-style facts: age, gender, income, location. Example: "Women 35-50." A media buying input, not a message, an ad at this depth speaks to no one in particular.

## First principles

A reasoning approach that breaks a problem down to its most fundamental, irreducible truths and builds the answer back up from there, rather than reasoning by analogy, convention, or "how it's usually done."

## Headline

The primary short line of text on an ad meant to communicate the core value proposition or hook at a glance.

## Hook

The opening seconds or first line of an ad, whatever grabs attention and earns the next few seconds of watch time before the core message is delivered.

## ICP

Ideal Customer Profile: the specific type of customer or account that gets the most value from the product and is the best fit for the business, used to focus targeting, messaging, and creative.

## Messaging angle

The specific argument or narrative lens used to position an offer to a given audience (e.g. leading with price, social proof, urgency, a pain point), distinct from the visual format carrying it.

## Micro-persona

That persona caught in a specific moment: life stage + role + circumstance + environment + emotional state + awareness stage, today and this week. Example: "Sunday night before the first week of school. IEP meeting Thursday. Kid already anxious at dinner. She's in bed at 11pm researching options because the cafeteria is the trigger, problem-aware sliding into solution-aware, burned by products that promised calm and didn't deliver." An ad at this depth makes them feel seen. The reaction it produces is "how did they know?"

## OpEx

Operating Expense: the ongoing cost of running day-to-day business operations, as opposed to one-time capital expenditure (CapEx).

## Persona

A person inside the segment, seen from the inside: their pains, triggers, objections, transformations, and voice. Example: "The Advocate Mom, mom of a teen with sensory processing disorder who's become the family's full-time caseworker: school meetings, therapist waitlists, decoding meltdowns." An ad at this depth resonates, it sounds like it knows them.

## Reason to believe

The proof that makes a claim credible. Whatever the ad offers so the viewer doesn't have to take the promise on faith: a mechanism ("how it works"), an ingredient or spec, a demonstration, a stat, social proof, an authority endorsement, or a guarantee. Distinct from the messaging angle (the argument being made): the angle is the claim, the reason to believe is why the claim is true. Example: angle is "falls asleep in minutes," the reason to believe is "drug-free magnesium blend developed with sleep researchers." Often shortened to RTB.

## Trigger moment

The moment a problem stops being tolerable. It's the moment that motivates someone to take action. This is what separates a persona who has a pain point in the abstract from a persona who is actually about to buy: the trigger moment is the specific event or accumulation that flips them from tolerating the problem to acting on it. It's what a micro-persona should be built around, since a micro-persona is a persona caught at (or near) their trigger moment.

## Visual format

The structural/stylistic pattern an ad's visuals follow (e.g. talking head, UGC testimonial, text-on-screen, meme, product demo) that shapes how the message gets delivered, independent of the specific message itself.
